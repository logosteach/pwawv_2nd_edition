::: anchored-in-christ
I want to take a moment and reflect on something we have learned today and try to make a connection with something that God reveals to us in His word. The word I am thinking of is *regression*. In today's lesson we learned that a regression is a bug that creeps back in, or something that breaks, right after we changed code we thought we were improving.[^1]

We are all like that in real life, aren't we? God is very clear and specific about the kind of life and liberty He has given and called us to in His Son. But many times we think we need to adjust, or fix things a little, because clearly, "God just doesn't understand that my case is just a little bit different. I cannot forgive that person. He just does not understand what they did to me." or perhaps, "If I don't go with the crowd, then all my friends are going to think I am strange. I might even get cancelled."[^2]

What a comfort it is to know that for all this, there is grace and forgiveness. God is working in His children, and despite our regressions, He will complete what He started.[^3]

> "This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast." (Hebrews 6:19, NKJV)

*See also: Proverbs 14:12; Galatians 5:1; Matthew 6:14–15; Colossians 3:13; Exodus 23:2; Romans 12:2; Lamentations 3:22–23; 1 John 1:9; Philippians 1:6.*
:::

## Theological Points

1. **Spiritual regression**: Just as a code regression reintroduces a defect after an apparent improvement, believers can reintroduce patterns of sin or self-justification even after receiving clear instruction from God’s Word. The standard remains God’s revealed will, not our adjusted version of it.[^1]

2. **The clarity of God’s call and the temptation to revise it**: God has spoken clearly about the life and liberty that belong to those who are in Christ. The temptation to treat our situation as a special exception (“God doesn’t understand my case”) is a form of self-justification that must be tested against Scripture.[^2]

3. **Persevering grace**: Despite our regressions, God is at work in His children and will complete the work He began. This is the comfort of the perseverance of the saints, grounded in God’s faithfulness rather than in our consistency.[^3]

4. **Hope as an anchor**: The believer’s hope in Christ is sure and steadfast (Hebrews 6:19). It holds the soul even when regressions occur, because it rests on Christ’s finished work and God’s unchanging purpose.

## Supporting Scripture References

### The danger of revising God’s clear Word
- Proverbs 14:12 — There is a way that seems right to a man, but its end is the way to death.
- Galatians 5:1 — For freedom Christ has set us free; stand firm and do not submit again to a yoke of slavery.
- Romans 12:2 — Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind.
- Exodus 23:2 — You shall not fall in with the many to do evil.

### Forgiveness and the life we are called to
- Matthew 6:14–15 — If you forgive others their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you.
- Colossians 3:13 — Bearing with one another and, if one has a complaint against another, forgiving each other; as the Lord has forgiven you, so you also must forgive.
- 1 John 1:9 — If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.

### God’s persevering work despite our regressions
- Philippians 1:6 — He who began a good work in you will bring it to completion at the day of Jesus Christ.
- Lamentations 3:22–23 — The steadfast love of the Lord never ceases; his mercies never come to an end; they are new every morning.
- Hebrews 6:19 — We have this as a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul.

## Footnotes

[^1]: In software testing, a regression is a defect that reappears after a change that was intended to improve the code. The spiritual parallel is the reappearance of sinful patterns or self-justifying reasoning even after we have received clear instruction from Scripture.

[^2]: God’s Word is clear about the life of liberty in Christ (Galatians 5:1) and about concrete duties such as forgiveness (Matthew 6:14–15; Colossians 3:13). The impulse to carve out an exception for ourselves is the same impulse Proverbs 14:12 warns against—the way that seems right to us.

[^3]: Philippians 1:6 and Lamentations 3:22–23 ground the comfort not in the believer’s ability to avoid regression, but in God’s faithful, persevering work. The hope of Hebrews 6:19 is anchored in Christ, not in our performance.
