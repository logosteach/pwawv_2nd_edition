# Anchored in Christ

## A Dangerous Prayer

In a limited sense, the prayer of Psalm 139 behaves a little bit like a unit test of the heart. When we run `unittest`, we are asking the test runner to search our code for hidden errors we might otherwise miss. David asks the Lord to do something similar: to search him, to try him, and to see if there is any wicked way in him. This has been called a dangerous prayer, and with good reason. When God searches us, however, He is not looking for something He does not already know. The opening of the psalm has already said that God knows David completely. The searching is for us. Discovering how much we rely on our own strength, and how far short we fall of God's glory, can be painful, and our pride rebels against it. Yet the ability to pray this prayer with a sincere heart is evidence that the gospel has found us and that God is at work in us. We need not become perfect, or even better, before we pray it. We come as we are, on the merit of Christ alone.[^1]

### Primary Verse

> "Search me, O God, and know my heart; Try me, and know my anxieties; And see if there is any wicked way in me, And lead me in the way everlasting."
>
> — Psalm 139:23–24, NKJV

---

## Theological Points

1. **Limited analogy**: The comparison between a unit test and David’s prayer is pedagogical and limited. `unittest` searches code for hidden errors the programmer may not have seen. God, however, already knows us completely. Psalm 139 is “A Psalm of David,” and verses 1–4 have already said that the LORD has searched and known him. The searching of verses 23–24 is for our good, not for God’s information.[^2]

2. **Falling short, and the pain of being searched**: Discovering how much we rely on our own strength, and how far short we fall of God’s glory, can be painful. Our pride rebels against it. The standard is the glory of God, not our own measure of ourselves.[^3]

3. **A sincere prayer is the fruit of grace**: Being able to pray this prayer before the Lord with a sincere heart means that the gospel has found us and that God is at work in us. The willingness to be searched is not a spiritual achievement we offer to God. It is the fruit of the good work He has already begun.[^4]

4. **Come as you are, on Christ’s merit**: We must not try to become perfect, or even better, before we pray this prayer. We come on Christ’s merit, not on how well we have first cleaned ourselves up. Self-examination is real and necessary, but it is never done in our own strength. Our hearts deceive us; we ask the Lord to search us, and we come to Him as we are.[^5]

- Psalm 139:1–4 — “O LORD, You have searched me and known me.” He understands our thought afar off and is acquainted with all our ways.

## Footnotes

[^1]: The analogy is intentionally limited. A unit test evaluates code against an objective standard and exposes hidden failure. In a comparable (but not identical) way, we ask the Lord to search us and to expose what is hidden in the heart. The parallel must not be pressed beyond this pedagogical point. Unlike a test suite, God already knows what He will find (Psalm 139:1–4).

[^2]: Primary text: Psalm 139:23–24. The superscription identifies the psalm as “A Psalm of David.” Verses 1–4 have already confessed that God knows David’s sitting down and rising up, his thoughts, his path, and every word on his tongue. Verses 23–24, then, are a request to be shown and led, not a request that God acquire information. See also Jeremiah 17:9–10; Hebrews 4:13; 1 Samuel 16:7; Psalm 44:21.

[^3]: Romans 3:23. See also Romans 3:10–12; Isaiah 64:6; Proverbs 16:18; James 4:6; 1 Peter 5:5. The pain of this discovery is not a sign that the prayer has failed. It is what happens when the glory of God, rather than the claims of pride, is allowed to be the standard.

[^4]: Philippians 1:6; Philippians 2:13. The ability to pray sincerely is God’s work, not ours. “The gospel has found us” is a way of saying that grace has already taken hold; we do not first make ourselves searchable and then come. See also Ephesians 2:8–9; Romans 5:8.

[^5]: 2 Corinthians 13:5 calls us to examine ourselves and to test ourselves, yet Jeremiah 17:9–10 reminds us that the heart is deceitful and that it is the LORD who searches it. We therefore come as we are, on Christ’s merit, and ask Him both to search us and to “lead [us] in the way everlasting” (Psalm 139:24). See also Hebrews 4:16; Psalm 26:2; Psalm 19:12; 1 Corinthians 11:28; Galatians 6:4.
