## Anchored in Christ

Our God tests. He tests all mankind and yes, even His children.[^1] The reality is, tests are not fun, are they? We don't like going through them. At least, very few of us do. Yet, they have immense value if endured patiently and faithfully.[^2] There is great joy waiting for us if we wait patiently during trials and testing and just remain faithful.[^3]

> "This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast." (Hebrews 6:19, NKJV)

*See also: Psalm 11:4–5; Proverbs 17:3; Jeremiah 17:10; Genesis 22:1; Hebrews 12:6–7, 11; Romans 5:3–4; James 1:2–4, 12; 1 Peter 1:6–7; Revelation 2:10.*
:::

---

## Theological Points

1. **God tests all mankind, including His children**: Just as `unittest` puts our functions to the test, Scripture teaches that God Himself tests. He examines all people, and He also tests those who belong to Him. This is not because God lacks knowledge. He already knows the heart. He tests in order to reveal, refine, and deal with us according to truth.[^1]

2. **Trials are painful, yet valuable when endured in faith**: Scripture does not pretend that testing is pleasant. “No chastening seems to be joyful for the present, but painful.” Afterward, however, it yields “the peaceable fruit of righteousness.” The testing of faith produces patience, character, and a genuineness more precious than gold.[^2]

3. **Joy is promised to those who endure**: There is great joy held out to those who wait patiently and remain faithful—the crown of life, which the Lord has promised to those who love Him. This is not a wage we earn by toughness. It is the Lord’s promised gift, and it is kept by His grace.[^3]

4. **Hope as an anchor**: In the midst of testing, the believer’s hope in Christ is “an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast” (Hebrews 6:19). That hope enters behind the veil, where Jesus has gone as our forerunner. We remain faithful because we are anchored in Him, not because we are strong enough to hold on alone.

## Supporting Scripture References

### God tests all mankind, and He tests His children
- Psalm 11:4–5 — His eyelids test the sons of men; the LORD tests the righteous.
- Jeremiah 17:10 — “I, the LORD, search the heart, I test the mind.”
- Proverbs 17:3 — The refining pot is for silver and the furnace for gold, but the LORD tests the hearts.
- Genesis 22:1 — God tested Abraham.
- Hebrews 12:6–7 — Whom the LORD loves He chastens; if you endure chastening, God deals with you as with sons.

### The painful value of trials endured in faith
- Hebrews 12:11 — “Now no chastening seems to be joyful for the present, but painful; nevertheless, afterward it yields the peaceable fruit of righteousness to those who have been trained by it.”
- James 1:2–4 — Count it all joy when you fall into various trials; the testing of your faith produces patience.
- Romans 5:3–4 — Tribulation produces perseverance; and perseverance, character; and character, hope.
- 1 Peter 1:6–7 — Though grieved by various trials, the genuineness of your faith, though tested by fire, may be found to praise, honor, and glory.

### The joy promised to the faithful, and the hope that anchors us
- James 1:12 — Blessed is the man who endures temptation; he will receive the crown of life which the Lord has promised to those who love Him.
- Revelation 2:10 — “Be faithful until death, and I will give you the crown of life.”
- Hebrews 6:19 — “This hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and steadfast.”

## Footnotes

[^1]: God tests “the sons of men” (Psalm 11:4) and “tests the righteous” (Psalm 11:5). He tested Abraham (Genesis 22:1). He searches the heart and tests the mind (Jeremiah 17:10; Proverbs 17:3). This testing is distinct from temptation to sin, which God does not do (James 1:13). In His children, testing often takes the form of fatherly discipline (Hebrews 12:6–7).

[^2]: Hebrews 12:11 states plainly that chastening is not joyful for the present, but painful. The value is in what it yields: the peaceable fruit of righteousness, patience (James 1:3), character and hope (Romans 5:3–4), and faith proven genuine (1 Peter 1:6–7). The call to endure patiently and faithfully is a call to perseverance, not a claim that we contribute the merit.

[^3]: James 1:12 and Revelation 2:10 promise the crown of life to those who endure and remain faithful. That joy is the Lord’s promised gift, and it is held by the sure and steadfast hope of Hebrews 6:19, which is anchored in Christ, not in our performance.
