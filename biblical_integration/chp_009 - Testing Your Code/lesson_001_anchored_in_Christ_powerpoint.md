# Anchored in Christ

## The Assertive Spirit of God

In a limited sense, the Holy Spirit behaves a little bit like an assert statement regarding God's gospel call to the world. When the Word is preached, the world's view of itself and of its standing before God is challenged. Sinners are reminded that they do not stand justly before a Holy God. This is part of the work of the Holy Spirit in the preaching of the Word. The conviction of sin, however, is not merely diagnostic but is part of the effectual call of God. A great contrast that we find between the work of the Spirit and the Python assert statement is that the Spirit is personal and active. He is not simply a feature of language or a force to be controlled and manipulated by the tongues of men and women.[^1]

### Primary Verse

> "And when He has come, He will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment."
>
> — John 16:8, NKJV

---

## Theological Points

1. **Limited analogy**: The comparison between the Holy Spirit and a Python `assert` statement is pedagogical and limited. It illustrates one aspect of the Spirit’s work (pressing an objective standard against a false claim) without reducing the Spirit to a language feature or mechanism.[^2]

2. **Conviction through the preached Word**: When the Word is preached, the Spirit challenges the world’s view of itself and of its standing before a holy God. Sinners are reminded that they do not stand justly before Him.[^3]

3. **Effectual call, not mere diagnosis**: The conviction of sin is not merely diagnostic or informational. In the elect it belongs to the effectual call of God, by which the Spirit sovereignly applies the benefits of Christ’s redemption.[^4]

4. **Personal and sovereign Spirit**: The Holy Spirit is a divine Person—active, personal, and free. He is not a force that can be controlled or manipulated by human technique, rhetoric, or “the tongues of men and women.”[^5]

## Supporting Scripture References

### Conviction of the world through the Word
- John 16:8–11 — The Spirit convicts the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment.
- Acts 2:37 — Hearers were “cut to the heart” when the Word was preached.
- Hebrews 4:12 — The Word is living and active, discerning the thoughts and intentions of the heart.
- 1 Thessalonians 1:5 — The gospel came in power and in the Holy Spirit and with full conviction.

### No one stands justly before a holy God
- Romans 3:10–12, 19–20, 23 — None is righteous; the law stops every mouth; all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.
- Isaiah 64:6 — All our righteous deeds are like a polluted garment.
- Psalm 143:2 — No one living is righteous before God.
- Romans 1:18–21 — God’s wrath is revealed; people suppress the truth.

### Effectual call and the Spirit’s sovereign work
- Romans 8:30 — Those whom he predestined he also called… justified… glorified.
- 1 Corinthians 1:23–24 — To those who are called, Christ is the power and wisdom of God.
- John 6:44, 65 — No one comes to Christ unless the Father draws him.
- 2 Thessalonians 2:13–14 — God chose you and called you through the gospel.
- John 3:3–8 — One must be born of the Spirit; the Spirit gives life sovereignly.
- Ephesians 2:1–5 — Dead in trespasses; made alive by God.
- 1 Corinthians 2:14 — The natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit.

### The Spirit is personal, free, and not under human control
- John 3:8 — The wind blows where it wishes; so it is with everyone born of the Spirit.
- 1 Corinthians 12:11 — The Spirit apportions gifts as he wills.
- John 14:16–17, 26; 15:26; 16:13–14 — Personal titles and actions of the Helper / Spirit of truth.
- Acts 16:14 — The Lord opened Lydia’s heart.
- Titus 3:5–6 — Regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit poured out richly.

## Footnotes

[^1]: The analogy is intentionally limited. An `assert` statement evaluates a condition against an objective standard and fails when the condition is false. In a comparable (but not identical) way, the Spirit applies the objective standard of Scripture to human claims. The parallel must not be pressed beyond this pedagogical point.

[^2]: See the Theological Points section above. The Spirit remains the third Person of the Trinity, not a construct of human language or programming.

[^3]: Primary text: John 16:8. See also Acts 2:37; Hebrews 4:12; 1 Thessalonians 1:5. The external call of the gospel goes out in the preaching of the Word; the Spirit makes that Word effective according to God’s purpose.

[^4]: The effectual (internal) call is distinguished from the external call heard by all. Supporting texts include Romans 8:30; 1 Corinthians 1:23–24; John 6:44; 2 Thessalonians 2:13–14; John 3:3–8; Ephesians 2:1–5. Mere exposure of sin (diagnostic) is not the same as the sovereign, life-giving work of the Spirit in the elect.

[^5]: John 3:8; 1 Corinthians 12:11; the personal language of John 14–16; Acts 16:14; Titus 3:5–6. The Spirit cannot be summoned, manipulated, or controlled by human eloquence or technique.
